Where that mnemonic misleads youThe C is constrained by draught, and it is not in the ladder. Rule 18(a) to (c) runs NUC, RAM, fishing, sail, power. CBD sits in Rule 18(d) as a separate, weaker duty: any vessel other than a NUC or a RAM shall, if circumstances admit, avoid impeding her safe passage. Avoid impeding is not keep out of the way. If a paper asks for the Rule 18 order, leave CBD out of it.

Learn it as three sentences, not a ladder

18(a) Power keeps out of the way of NUC, RAM, fishing, sail.  18(b) Sail keeps out of the way of NUC, RAM, fishing.  18(c) Fishing keeps out of the way of NUC, RAM. That is the whole hierarchy, and it is shorter than the mnemonic.

Constrained by draughtA power-driven vessel: deep, not restricted. Rule 3(h) needs draught against available depth and width. Her signals are optional: Rule 28 says she may show three all-round reds or a cylinder, so an absent cylinder proves nothing. And she carries full Rule 23(a) power-driven lights underway, masthead lights included.
Motoring coneA sailing yacht under engine is power-driven, black cone point down by day. There is no under-12m exemption internationally. That exemption exists only in the US Inland and Canadian rules, so do not repeat it on an RYA paper.
The clause that beats the whole ladderRule 18 opens "except where Rules 9, 10 and 13 otherwise require". Narrow channels, traffic schemes and overtaking all override it. Rule 13(a) is worded "notwithstanding anything contained in Rules 4 to 18", so an overtaking vessel keeps clear whatever she is: a yacht overtaking a trawler gives way. Note the exact limit, though. Rule 13 does not override Rule 19, so in restricted visibility there is no overtaking vessel in the Rule 13 sense.
Plate II

The steering rules

Rule 13 · Overtaking

Coming from more than 22.5° abaft her beam. At night you see only her sternlight, no sidelight. Once overtaking, always overtaking, until finally past and clear.

Rule 14 · Head-on

Power vessels only. Both alter to starboard, pass port to port. Applies when in doubt as well as when certain.

Rule 15 · Crossing

Power vessels only. The one with the other on her starboard side gives way, and shall avoid crossing ahead.

Rule 12 · Sail against sail

1. Different tacks: port gives way to starboard. 2. Same tack: windward gives way to leeward. 3. On port tack, seeing a vessel to windward and unsure of her tack: give way.

Your tack is the side opposite the boom.

Rule 10 · Crossing a traffic separation scheme

Rule 10(c) actually begins "shall, so far as practicable, avoid crossing traffic lanes". Only if obliged to cross: cross on a heading as nearly as practicable at right angles to the general direction of traffic flow. You do not offset for tide. The skewed track is intended: your aspect stays obvious to traffic in the lane, which is the whole point. Correcting for tide is the wrong answer on the paper and the wrong thing at sea.

10(j) under 20m or sailing: do not impede a power-driven vessel following a lane. 10(i) fishing: do not impede any vessel following a lane. 10(d)(i) those same three may use the inshore traffic zone. A permission, not an instruction.

Rule 9 · Narrow channels

Keep to the starboard side (9(a)). Do not anchor in it (9(g)). Do not cross it if crossing impedes a vessel that can only navigate within it (9(d)).

The not-impede duties are two rules with two scopes, and that difference is examined. 9(b) under 20m or sailing: do not impede a vessel that can safely navigate only within the channel. 9(c) fishing: do not impede any other vessel navigating within the channel. Stricter, and no "only within" qualifier.

Rule 17, standing onMaintain course and speed. 17(a)(ii): you may act when the give-way vessel clearly is not. 17(b): you shall act when collision can no longer be avoided by her alone. 17(c) is narrower than usually quoted: it binds a power-driven vessel taking 17(a)(ii) action in a crossing situation with another power-driven vessel, and says do not alter to port for a vessel on your own port side. Not sail, not head-on, not overtaking.
Rule 19, restricted visibilityThere is no stand-on vessel. Nobody has right of way. On radar alone, avoid altering to port for a vessel forward of the beam, and avoid altering towards one abeam or abaft the beam.

Making way: the three cases are not the same

The special lights show whether making way or not. The ordinary navigation lights appear only when making way, and this is where people over-generalise.

NUCRule 27(a)(iii)
adds sidelights and sternlight. No masthead light.
RAMRule 27(b)(iii)
adds masthead light(s), sidelights and sternlight.
FishingRule 26(b)(iii), (c)(iii)
adds sidelights and sternlight. A trawler of 50m or more also carries a masthead light abaft of and above the green.
CBDRule 28
carries full Rule 23(a) power-driven lights at all times underway. The three reds are an optional extra on top.
Underway vs making wayUnderway means not anchored, not aground, not made fast to the shore. It does not mean moving. Making way means moving through the water. A drifting yacht with no engine and no way on is underway but not making way.
Small-vessel exemptionsRule 27(g): under 12m need not show the NUC/RAM lights or shapes, except when diving. Rule 30(e): under 7m at anchor, clear of channels and normal traffic, need not show an anchor light. Rule 30(f): under 12m aground need not show the aground lights or shapes.

Rule 35(d), the anchor exceptionA vessel engaged in fishing when at anchor, and a RAM carrying out her work at anchor, sound the 35(c) signal (one prolonged, two short) instead of the anchor bell. So "at anchor" does not automatically mean bell.
Small-vessel exemptions35(i): 12m up to 20m need not give the bell signals, but must then make some other efficient sound signal every 2 minutes. 35(j): under 12m need not give any of these signals, same 2-minute substitute. Rule 33(a) sets the kit: whistle from 12m, bell from 20m, gong from 100m.

Timing itThe period is the whole cycle including the dark. Start the watch at the first flash of a group and stop it at the first flash of the next group.
Two different rangesNominal range in the almanac assumes 10M visibility. Rising and dipping range is geometry: height of light plus height of eye. From a yacht's low eye height, the geometric limit is often the smaller of the two.
Plate VII

Definitions & last-hour recall

UnderwayNot at anchor, not made fast to the shore, not aground. It does not mean moving.
Making wayMoving through the water. Different thing entirely.
In sight of one anotherOnly when one can be seen visually from the other. A radar contact is not in sight, which is exactly why Rule 19 exists.
Risk of collisionExists if the compass bearing of an approaching vessel does not appreciably change. Assume it exists in any doubt, and beware a close tow or a very large vessel even when the bearing does change.
Engaged in fishingGear that restricts manoeuvrability. Trolling lines do not count.
Restricted visibilityFog, mist, falling snow, heavy rainstorms, sandstorms or anything similar.

If you only revise eight things

  1. Rule 18 as its three paragraphs (a), (b), (c), with CBD sitting outside them in 18(d) as a not-impede duty.
  2. Rule 12: port gives way to starboard, windward gives way to leeward.
  3. Rule 13 overtaking overrides Rules 4 to 18, but not Rule 19.
  4. Rule 10: avoid crossing a TSS if you can, and if you must, cross on a heading at right angles. Do not offset for tide.
  5. Cardinal topmarks and the clock-face flash counts.
  6. Isolated danger versus safe water versus emergency wreck.
  7. The five light mnemonics: red/red, red/white/red, red/white, white/red, green/white.
  8. Fog signals for anchored, for the awkward-vessel set, and the 35(d) exception for fishing and RAM at anchor.
  9. Oc versus Fl versus Iso, and what a period actually measures.
